# Tempo Calendar — Chrome & Firefox extension

A full-page calendar that syncs with Google Calendar and auto-schedules long tasks into your free time.

## Features
- **Google Calendar sync**: see, create, edit, drag, resize and delete events across all your calendars. Supports time zones (per event and display), recurring events (daily / weekly on chosen days / monthly by date or "2nd Tuesday" / "last Friday" / yearly, with an end date or count), and edits or deletes scoped to *this event*, *this and following*, or *all events*. Also notifications (popup and email, several per event), guests, Google Meet, location, color, busy/free and visibility.
- **From moves To**: wherever there's a start and an end — the event dialog, quick create, the task dialog (don't start before → deadline), project steps, leave days, the custom export range and Analysis' custom period — typing or picking a new start moves the end with it, so the length stays the same. The −/+ buttons are different: each changes only its own field (see below).
- **From / To**: in the event dialog the start (*From*) and end (*To*) are on separate lines, each with its own −/+ buttons underneath (a small **−** and **+**: tap one and the steps show for a moment — 5, 10, 15, 30 min and 1 h, the same both ways (15 min, 1 h, 1 day for dates) — and fold away a few seconds after the last tap; under *From* they change only the start, under *To* only the end — the end is pushed on only if the start would pass it) and *Now*, *Since last event* / *Till next event*. The task dialog has *From* (don't start before) and *To* (deadline) lines the same way, with −1d … +1d buttons — also each changing only its own field (*From* never moves the deadline, unless it would come within a day of it). Quick create's −/+ buttons work the same way.
- **Compact dialogs**: the event and task dialogs show only the essentials (title, times or hours, deadline, rank, and category with color on one row). Everything else sits in sections that start collapsed and open with the ▾ arrow, each with a one-line summary of its current settings: for events *Notifications & repeat* and *Calendar, location & more* (time zone, calendar, busy/free, visibility, location, guests, Meet); for tasks *Notifications & repeat*, *Project & order*, *Sessions* and *When to schedule*. The Settings tabs are grouped the same way (e.g. Scheduling → Work hours, Time periods, Task sessions, Planning, Limits…).
- **Week view** shows the calendar week by default, or *Today + next 6 days* (Settings → General → Week view shows). 
- **Visual editing**: drag across empty time to create an event; drag across the all-day row or month cells for multi-day events; drag to move, drag the bottom edge to resize. Keyboard: `D/W/M/Y` switch views (day, week, month, analysis), `R` refreshes from Google, `T` jumps to today, `←/→` move between periods, `C` makes a new event, `A` makes a new task, `Q` quick add, `V` weekly review, `Ctrl/Cmd+Z` or `U` undo.
- **Splittable tasks**: give the total hours, a deadline and a rank. Tempo splits the task into sessions (60 min by default, adjustable per task and in Settings) and places them in free time inside your day bounds (9–5 by default, adjustable per task and in Settings). Optional per-task settings: max minutes per day, break between sessions, shortest session, "don't start before", and a reminder.
- **Ranking**: rank 1 is the most important. Higher-ranked tasks get the earliest free time, so adding a rank-2 task pushes rank-3 sessions to later slots automatically.
- **Reading plans**: set the pages to read, the start page and your speed (pages per hour, pages per minute or minutes per page). Example: 200 pages of *Pride and Prejudice* from page 47 at 20 pages/hour gives 10 sessions labelled `Pride and Prejudice page 47-66, task 1/10` … `page 227-246, task 10/10`. You can switch the labels to relative numbering (page 1-20…).
- **Sidebar**: the *Calendars* list folds away with a click on its ▾ header (it shows how many calendars are on); remembered on this device.
- **Habits** (in the *Goals* tab, and the sidebar's *Habits* tab; modelled on the Loop Habit Tracker for Android): each habit has a name, a question, a colour and a frequency — every day, N times a week or N times a month — or is *measurable* with a target per day, week or month (e.g. 8 glasses a day, 20 km a week). The list shows a score ring (habit strength, which rises as you keep the habit and decays slowly when you don't, with weekly habits judged over a rolling week) and the last days, today first: click a day to tick it off (✓) or untick it (✕), or to enter the amount for a measurable habit; right-click or long-press to mark it skipped (») — a skipped day doesn't break a streak. **Automatic habits** (yes/no habits, *Edit → Automatic, from your calendar*): give some words, and Tempo ticks the habit by itself — either *on days when an event or task contains one of them* (e.g. “run, gym” ticks Exercise when “Morning run” is on your calendar, and leaves the day unticked otherwise), or *every day except when one contains them* (e.g. “computer games” unticks “Didn't play computer games” on the day of “Computer games with Max”). Only events and task sessions that have already started count, never future ones (so today is decided as the day goes on); words match the start of a word, ignoring case and accents (“run” finds “Running club” but not “Brunch”). Automatic days have a small dot and their tooltip says which event did it (⚡ marks the habit); click a day to set it yourself — that wins — and click again to hand it back. A third kind ticks a habit **by hours in categories**: *at least* (or *at most*) so many hours a day — or a week (“at least” ticks the day the week's total gets there, “at most” each day while it stays under) — in the categories you pick, e.g. “Not more than 2 h of screen time”. A fourth ticks a habit **by time of day**: on days when something that counts (titles with some words and/or categories) *starts by* or *ends before* a time — e.g. “end sleep before 07:00” (ticked when your sleep ended by 7, not when you were still asleep at 7; split sleep blocks count for the morning they end) or “finish the essay before 11:00”; a day whose time hasn't come yet waits. All of this sits in the habit's *Advanced: automatic tracking* block, which can also limit it to some hours (*Only count what happens during*: day bounds, outside them, waking hours or a time period — e.g. “Read 1 h” counting only reading after hours); goal rules (*Advanced: complete automatically*) have the same choice. It looks back up to 60 days (from the date you choose) and updates after every refresh. Click a habit for its page: score, month and year change, total, a score chart, history per week, a calendar of the last 20 weeks (click a day to change it), best streaks and how often it's done on each weekday; *Edit* changes it, archives or deletes it. Habits sync to your other devices. In game mode a habit earns life XP when ticked off and small **streak rewards** — +1 XP for each day (or week/month) in a row, up to 6 at the first rank (one more per rank), 4% more per rank, plus milestones at 7, 30, 100 and 365 days (4, 12, 52 weeks); untick *Counts in game mode* in the habit's dialog to leave it out. The weekly review has a *Gewohnheiten* section: what was kept against the frequency, score and its change, the current streak, and a square per day.
- **Game mode** (Settings → General → *Game mode*): Tempo becomes a game — earn experience points and level up from 1 to 300, through 16 ranks each with its own emblem (🌱 Seedling, 🌿 Sprout, 📒 Apprentice Planner at 20, 🧭 Pathfinder, 🛠️ Taskwright, ⏳ Timekeeper at 50, 📜 Chronicler, 🔮 Chronomancer at 100, 🛡️ Rhythm Knight, 🦉 Habit Sage, ⌛ Hourglass Warden, ♟️ Grand Strategist, 🧙 Archmage of Hours, 🏛️ Eternal Architect, 🌌 Celestial Navigator, 👑 Master of Time). XP comes in three colours:
  - <b>App XP</b> (blue) for using Tempo: opening it each day (more for a streak), scheduling events and tasks (a fifth of what they're worth — the rest comes when you do them), starting projects, logging habits, achievements.
  - <b>Busy XP</b> (orange) for getting things done: events (longer ones earn more; ticked off ×1.2), task sessions (each later session of a task is worth 8% more), finished tasks (+40 and more) and projects (+200), a *full day* (at least half of 7:00–23:00 planned with things other than sleep), keeping to the plan, and a weekly bonus for how much of the week was planned.
  - <b>Life XP</b> (green) for time in the *life-growth* categories you choose (reading, working out…), a little for sleep and very little for leisure (1.5 an hour, against 30 for life growth), habits, and goals.
  - Each item counts mostly as one kind: a life-growth or leisure item earns life XP and only a little busy XP (15% by default); a work item earns busy XP; scheduling earns only a small share (5%) of what an item is worth. Categories you set to *never score* (Settings → Game mode) earn nothing.
  - **Life XP is what counts in the long run**: life XP is for actions that transform you or move your life goals forward. You won't spend many hours a week on them, so choose the life-growth categories carefully — and even short spells are valued highly: life XP grows with hours^0.7 (30 min = 31, 1 h = 50, 2 h = 81 by default), and it never wears off. Everything else — busy time, leisure, sleep, using the app, the weekly bonus — **settles**: full value for your first 7 days of regular use, then shrinking (about a third after a month, down to 15%). The game panel shows the current factor; the day breakdown marks settled lines (×0.39). After a month, levelling up on busy time alone is slow — a higher level means you've grown, not just kept busy. All values are adjustable under *Advanced*.
  - **Weighting life-growth categories**: under the life-growth categories, set how much each counts — e.g. *Study* at 50% of normal life XP (main categories pass their weight to sub-categories). **Finishing** a task gives busy XP plus a little life XP (10 + 2 per session), a project 200 busy + 60 life XP — once, on the day it was first finished; it never counts again, even if it's reopened and finished later.
  - **Road to level 300** (top of *Ranks, achievements & XP log*; with knobs for how many weeks of your data it goes by (1–26), extra life growth a week, and life growth / everything else compared to now — the chart redraws as you change them): a chart of your level over the coming years if the last four weeks are your normal pace (no drains), with the dates you'd reach levels 10, 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, 200, 250 and 300 — and a second line showing how much sooner with 2 h more life growth a week.
  - **XP drains** (Settings → Game mode → *XP drains*): activities that cost XP, e.g. *Gameplay* — titles with some words and/or categories, per week or per day. You set the free hours, how many hours are tolerated and what they cost (a % of the XP of each day you did it), how much more each hour beyond costs (a % of the whole week's — or day's — XP), the maximum (75% by default), and which kinds of XP it takes (app, busy, life). E.g. 1.5 h of gaming in a week: −10% of that day's XP; 4 h: 2 h over → 10% + 2 × 15% = −40% of the week's XP. Each drain explains itself in a sentence; the day breakdown and the game panel show what was drained. Changing drains (or the life-growth, leisure and never-score categories, or the advanced values) counts all your XP again from the start.
  - **Drains scale with your rank**: each rank (every 5 levels) makes drains cost 3% more (adjustable). Under each drain, a small chart shows what every hour would cost you right now — based on your recent daily and weekly XP — and, in green, what you'd win if nothing is detected.
  - **Clean bonus**: a day without any XP drain earns 4% of that day's XP, +0.2% for each clean day in a row; a week without any drain earns 5% of the week's XP, +0.5% per clean week in a row. The bonus is capped by rank: 6% at the first rank, +0.25% per rank, never more than 15% (all adjustable under *Advanced*). Days when no drain is in force earn no clean bonus. The game panel shows your clean streak (days and weeks), the cap at your rank and what keeping today clean is worth; a message tells you each morning when the streak grew — or broke. The day breakdown lists the bonus.
  - **Drains never score**: an event or session that counts as an XP drain (its category or words) earns no XP itself; the settings list the drain categories under *Categories that never score*.
  - **Win back drained XP**: XP lost to drains comes back with clean days in a row after the drain day — a share each day, *in trust*: after 5 clean days 75% is yours for good, after 10 all of it plus a 5% bonus. A drain before day 5 takes back everything given in trust; one between day 5 and 10 keeps the 75% and takes back the rest. Each new drain needs one more clean day for both steps (6 and 11, then 7 and 12…). The game panel shows what's being won back, the clean days so far, the days left and what's in trust; the day breakdown lists each day's share. All numbers are adjustable under *Advanced*.
  - **Which hours a drain watches** (a drain's *Advanced* block): all days and times (default), within the day bounds, outside them (“after hours”), the waking hours, or one of your time periods — e.g. only gaming on weekdays during work hours counts.
  - **Drain strength**: each drain has a *Strength (% of the costs)* — 100% by default; e.g. 25% for an activity that should only cost a little. (Bonus activities set how much extra they earn with *Extra XP %*.)
  - **For a while only**: each drain — and each **bonus activity** — counts *always*, for the *next week*, *next two weeks*, *next month*, *next 3 months* or a *custom period* (from–until dates); the editor says how many days are left, or when it ended. **Bonus activities** (titles with some words and/or categories) earn extra XP while they last — +50% by default — and score even in a never-score category.
  - **Category pickers** in the game settings work like the goals' ones: the chosen categories show as chips, *Choose categories…* opens the chips to pick from, *Done* folds them away.
  - **🚩 Level flags**: the calendar (week, day and month views) marks each day you reached a new level.
  - **Fireworks once per level**: the highest level celebrated is kept with your settings, so reopening the app (or another device) doesn't celebrate the same level again; a recount never does.
  - **Game news in the newspaper** (*Spielberichte*): level, rank emblem, XP that week/month, best day, levels reached, clean streak and bonuses, XP drained, achievements.
  - *Advanced* (in Settings → Game mode) lets you adjust the values: the level pace, XP per hour for events, task sessions, life growth, sleep and leisure, the cross-over share, the bonus for later sessions, scheduling, daily opening and streaks, full days, keeping to the plan, the rescheduling penalty, the weekly bonus, quest and goal XP, and how much life XP levelling up can ask for. Changing them counts all your days again.
  - **Goals** earn XP too: defining one (app XP), and reaching it (life XP) — more for longer periods (week < month < year) and for goals that need more hours (their time budget or "at least" hours rule), and ×1.5 for a goal set before its period begins that doesn't repeat (repeating copies earn a bit less). When a goal asks for hours, half of its value comes bit by bit as you put the hours in, the rest when it's reached. Each goal shows what it's worth (*🎮 +135*), and the goal dialogs show what it would earn as you fill them in.
  Rescheduling costs XP (moving or deleting a session, marking it undone, a session pushed later, dragging an event to another time) — more each time the same task slips again within a week. Levels come quicker at first (a busy week reaches about level 10–15; level 50 takes a couple of busy months) and slower afterwards; going up also needs a share of life XP, which grows slowly but never passes a quarter. Daily quests (complete three things, an hour of life growth, all habits ticked) and 13 achievements add XP. The sidebar's game panel (fold it with its header) shows your emblem, level, progress to the next, XP by type, streak, today's XP and quests; each day's header shows the XP earned (and, today and ahead, what's still to earn) — click it for the breakdown: busy, life and app XP item by item, rescheduling, quests and what's still to earn; an event's or session's popup says what completing it earns. Reaching a new level sets off fireworks (bigger for a new rank). Past days are settled into a ledger that syncs with your settings.
- **Sleep blocks** (Settings → General → Sleep): each night that has passed gets *😴 Sleep* events in your sleep hours (e.g. 23:00–07:00), fitted around anything already there — an event from 2–3 am gives 23:00–02:00 and 03:00–07:00. On your main calendar or only in this browser; added once per night (also across devices). They take the colour of your sleep category (on Google too); any event with a category but no colour of its own shows in its category's colour.
- **Task dialog details**: the deadline shortcuts (*End of work day*, *End of work week*, *Within two weeks*) count from *From* (don't start before) when it's set and later than now — a task from Wed 4 Nov gets Fri 6 Nov for the end of the work week. *Starts only after…* lists only tasks that aren't finished yet (repeating tasks and ones already chosen stay).
- **Newspaper layout**: the lower sections (goals, projects, habits, game news, quote and joke, outlook, corrections) are arranged by their heights — two of similar height side by side, the longer one wider (half/half, ⅓/⅔ or ⅔/⅓) so they end level, and a long section across the whole page in two or three columns of its own. It's redone when the window changes size and before printing; phones keep one column.
- **Projects**: **+ Project** (in the sidebar, or the + menu on phones) creates a chain of steps — tasks (split into sessions between their ideal start and end date, with the hours they need) and events (put on your calendar) — in the order they happen. Reorder steps with ▲▼, change a step between task and event, and add or remove steps later (removing one deletes its task or event). The project's name is added to each step's title — at the start (*Thesis: Write chapter 1*), at the end (*Write chapter 1 — Thesis*) or not at all. With *Steps run in order*, a task starts only after the previous task is done and never before an earlier event in the chain has ended. The sidebar's **Projects** tab shows each project's progress (steps done, hours done of the total, projected finish, the next step, a dot per step); click one to edit it. The weekly review has a *Projekte* section (time put in during the period, steps finished, progress and what's next, a headline when a project is completed), and Analysis can group time *by project*. A project can have a **category**: its tasks and events get it (new ones, ones without a category, and ones still on the project's previous category), and so does a task that joins the project from the task dialog without a category of its own; a project created from the task dialog takes that task's category.
- **Events inside events**: record something you did during another event — e.g. an hour of chores during an afternoon of childcare — with **＋ Add during this** in the event's (or a session's) popup, or by dragging in the free strip at the right edge of a day column. In Analysis the shorter, overlapping entry counts for its time (chores 1 h, childcare the remaining 3 h), so nothing is counted twice.
- **Task finished here**: click any session of a task and choose 🏁 *Task finished here* to declare the whole task complete in that session — it counts all remaining work (for reading: up to the last page), and the sessions not done yet are removed (here and on Google Calendar). A repeating task ends with that session.
- **Leave days**: click the weekday name above a day (week or day view) and pick 🏖 *On leave* or 🤒 *Sick leave*; for several days use *Settings → Scheduling → Leave days*. The day shows the label under its date and a light hatching, and task sessions are not planned on it. If the day already has events or task sessions, Tempo asks whether to reschedule them (default *Yes*: sessions move to other days, your own events move to the next working day at the same time; or only the sessions; or keep everything, which pins the sessions). A time period can *include leave days* (tick it in Settings → Scheduling → Time periods), and a single task can too (*When to schedule → Also schedule on my leave days*).
- **Series actions**: click a session to **mark it done**, **mark it undone** (its work goes back into the plan and later sessions shift), **delete it** (later sessions shift down), or **delete the entire series**. Drag a session to pin it to a time; the rest of the series reflows around it. Sessions whose time has passed count as done unless you mark them undone.
- **Not enough time?** Tempo prompts you to set a new deadline (it suggests the earliest one that fits), to ignore day bounds for that task (it then also uses evenings and weekends, 07:00–23:00 by default), or to keep the deadline and leave the overflow marked as overdue. The task dialog shows a live preview and warns you before you save.
- **Export**: the current week or month as **PDF**, **PNG**, **JPEG** or **TIFF**. **Several weeks or months** (up to 52 pages) export as one multi-page **PDF** or multi-page **TIFF** (Export → *Several weeks or months…*).
- **Analysis view** (press `Y`): shows how much of the next 7 or 30 days, this or next week, this or next month, or a custom range is free or occupied. You can measure it **within day bounds**, **outside day bounds**, over extended hours, or over the full 24 hours. Occupied time is broken down by **color category** (you can rename colors, e.g. Indigo → "Deep work"), by **task or calendar**, or by **events vs tasks**. The view has summary figures, a share bar, a per-day stacked chart, a table and a CSV download. Over the full 24 hours, 23:00–07:00 counts as **sleep (assumed)** on nights with no event or task in that time (nights you logged yourself use your entries); the page says so, and you can change the hours, pick which category it counts as (e.g. Personal, or its own *Sleep* group), or turn it off right there or in Settings → General.
- **Google is the source of truth for tasks**: every session event stores its task's settings, so a fresh install, another browser or another computer rebuilds the full task list (ranks, deadlines, done/undone, reading pages) straight from Google Calendar. Edits and deletions made on one device show up on the others. Tasks are found in whichever of your calendars they live in (e.g. a dedicated "Tempo Tasks" calendar), and each task remembers its calendar. Once loaded, a series can be fully edited from any browser: rank, deadline, done/undone, delete one session or the whole series. Use the **⟳ refresh** button next to the date (or press `R`) to re-read everything from Google right away. Tempo also refreshes on startup, every 5 minutes, and when you come back to the tab.
- **Categories for events too**: pick a category when you create an event (also when dragging) or later from any event's popup. It's stored on the event in Google, so it shows on every device; events you can't edit keep their category on that device.
- **Categories**: tag tasks as Work, Personal, Leisure, Study, or a category you add on the spot (in the task dialog, or from a session's popup). Each category has its own color, which Google Calendar uses too. Analysis can group time by category. Manage them in Settings → Categories.
- **Smart time allocation**: sessions normally use your session length, but a free gap up to the *wiggle room* shorter (15 min by default, so a 48-minute gap for 60-minute sessions) is used too, and a small leftover is folded into the last session. Adjustable in Settings → Scheduling and per task.
- **Time periods**: besides your day bounds, define named windows such as **After hours** (by default weekday evenings 17:30–22:00 and weekends 10:00–20:00). Each period has one or more rules (times + days, e.g. only Mon, Wed and Thu), edited in Settings → Scheduling → Time periods. Changing a rule's start keeps its end when that's still up to 3 h later, otherwise sets it an hour after the start (18:00 → 19:00). In the task dialog, choose *When to schedule*: work hours, any period, or custom hours for that task.
- **Limits**: Tempo asks before creating more than 20 sessions or repeats at once, refuses more than 50, and refuses tasks or repeating events that run more than 365 days ahead. For a repeating event over a limit it offers to end the series at the last allowed date. All three are adjustable in Settings → Scheduling → Limits (0 = no limit).
- **Category icons**: each category can have an emoji (💼 Work, 🏠 Personal, 🎉 Leisure, 📚 Study by default; pick from about 175 in the grid or type any). Colors: 26 swatches plus a custom color picker (the first 11 match Google Calendar's colors exactly; others show in Google as the nearest one). The icon starts every session title, e.g. `📚 Pride and Prejudice page 47-66, task 1/10`, also in Google Calendar, and shows on categorised events.
- **Categories sync between devices**: categories (names, colors, icons) and time periods are stored in one hidden all-day event dated 1 Jan 2000 on your primary calendar ("Tempo settings — please keep"), so every device and the phone app share them. The last change wins; a device syncing for the first time keeps its own customised categories.
- **Partly done**: click a session → *Partly done…*. For a book, enter the pages you read; for other tasks, how much of the session's work got done. Then choose whether the rest gets **extra time** (longer or added sessions) or is **fitted into the remaining sessions** (same slots, more pages in each). If something else took part of the slot, e.g. a 20-minute call, Tempo splits the block: a 20-minute event (or time counted toward another task) and a 40-minute reading session labelled `page 47-53, task 1/6 (partly done)`. This syncs to your other devices.
- **Fast, verified sync**: Tempo sends up to 6 requests to Google at once, backing off automatically when Google rate-limits or has a temporary error. Every insert carries its own ID, so a retry never creates a duplicate. After writing, Tempo reads everything back and rewrites anything missing or different, up to two more rounds. Sessions changed or deleted directly in Google are restored on the next sync. New and edited events are read back too. The sync panel shows *N of N sessions verified on Google*.
- **Repeating tasks**: e.g. *3 hours of exercise every week* or *30 minutes of reading every day* (optionally only on chosen weekdays), ending on a date or after a number of weeks or days. Each week or day is planned on its own, 4 weeks ahead by default (Settings → Scheduling → Planning). Sessions are labelled `Exercise · week of 19 Oct 2/3`.
- **Preferred times of day per category**: e.g. Study in the mornings, Leisure in the evenings (Settings → Categories). Tasks use their category's preferred time first and other times only when needed. You set what counts as morning, afternoon and evening yourself.
- **Daily cap**: the most task time per day across all tasks together (Settings → Scheduling → Planning).
- **Safety margin (soft vs hard deadline)**: *Aim to finish N days early* per task, or as a default. Tempo plans to finish by the target date and only uses the margin when it must. Sessions inside the margin are flagged (◔), and the deadline itself stays the hard limit.
- **Order and projects**: a task can *start only after* other tasks are done. Group tasks into a project whose steps run in order. A prerequisite inherits the urgency of the tasks waiting for it, and the sidebar groups each project's steps under it.
- **Travel buffer**: 15 minutes (adjustable) is blocked before and after events at a physical place. Online meetings get no buffer, and the buffer is shown as a hatched strip.
- **Session timer**: ▶ Timer (stopwatch) or 🍅 Focus (25/5 Pomodoro with break alerts) on any session. Stop early and *Partly done* is filled in from the time worked. Stop at or after the planned time and the session is marked done with the actual time.
- **Learns your reading speed** from the pages you report and the time spent (timer or the session's time on the calendar). It adjusts the plan without shifting pages already read. Turn this off in Settings → Scheduling.
- **Plan vs actual** (Analysis → *Plan vs actual*): per-week done, partly done and slipped sessions (deleted, marked undone, or imminent sessions pushed later), plus planned vs actual time and overruns per category.
- **Plain-language quick add** (box at the top of the sidebar, `Q`), e.g. `Read Dune 200 pages by Friday, Study`, `Exercise 3h per week for 10 weeks, Personal`, `Polish slides 2h by tomorrow 3pm after Draft rank 1`, `Tax return 4h by Nov 15 2 days early in the evenings`. A preview shows what was understood; press Enter to add, or *Edit details…*.
- **Undo** (↶, `Ctrl/Cmd+Z`, or the toast after a change): works for task changes (including deleting a whole series, whose sessions are put back on Google), single events (create, edit, move, delete, category) and settings.
- **What moved and why**: after a change, a toast and a *↷ moved* pill list the tasks pushed later, by how much, and which tasks took their time.
- **All settings sync between devices**: work hours, periods, limits, notification choices, categories, projects, planning options and history. Each setting merges on its own.
- **Other calendars** (Settings → Other calendars): another Google account, Outlook / Microsoft 365, iCloud (browser extension only), or any calendar link (.ics, webcal://). Their events are shown read-only and count as busy time, each calendar with its own *show* and *busy* switch. Outlook needs a one-time free Microsoft app registration; the steps are shown in Settings and in `WEB_APP.md`.
- **Date shortcuts**: every date and date-time field has a ⏱ menu with *Now*, *+1 hour*, *+1 day* and *+1 week*. On an event's start, the end moves with it, so the length stays the same.
- **Printouts with a time breakdown**: exported weeks and months (PDF, PNG, JPEG, TIFF, multi-page) end with a *Where the time goes* panel. It shows occupied vs free time, the busiest day, task sessions done, and a share bar with hours per category. It uses the same hours (e.g. within day bounds) and grouping as the Analysis view.
- **New events go to your task calendar** (Settings → Google Calendar → *Calendar for task sessions and new events*); you can still pick another calendar per event.
- **Done / undone for events**: click any event and choose *✓ Mark done*; it gets a tick and a faded look. *↺ Mark undone* reverses it (Undo works too). Events you can edit keep the mark in Google Calendar, so it shows on all your devices; read-only events (someone else's calendar) keep it on this device. Task sessions already had *Mark done / Mark undone*.

## Connect Google Calendar
### Option A — guided wizard (always available)
Go to ⚙ Settings → Google Calendar → **Set up Google Calendar**. The wizard links straight to each Google Cloud page, gives you the redirect URI to copy, checks the client ID you paste (it warns you if you pasted the client *secret*), connects, and tells you exactly what went wrong if it fails. It takes about 5 minutes, once.

The steps it walks you through (you can also do them by hand):
1. Create a project at <https://console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate>.
2. Enable the **Google Calendar API** for that project.
3. Set up the consent screen as **External**, then add your own Gmail under **Audience → Test users**.
4. Create an OAuth client of type **Web application**. Under *Authorized redirect URIs*, paste:
   - Chrome: `https://adheclccdehmdhjjfklageapjifndojo.chromiumapp.org/`
   - Firefox: copy it from Settings (it looks like `https://<hash>.extensions.allizom.org/`). One client can list both URIs.
5. Paste the **Client ID** and click **Connect**. On Google's screen, click *Continue* past "unverified app", then **tick every checkbox**.

### Option B — one-click "Sign in with Google" for everyone using your build
Google doesn't allow any calendar app to work without being registered once. If you do that registration once as the *publisher*, every user of your build gets a single **Sign in with Google** button:
1. In your Google Cloud project (set up as above), create:
   - an OAuth client of type **Chrome extension** with Item ID `adheclccdehmdhjjfklageapjifndojo`. This uses Chrome's own account picker, and tokens renew automatically.
   - an OAuth client of type **Web application** with both redirect URIs (for Firefox, Edge and Brave).
2. Put the two IDs in `src/js/config.js` (`CHROME_CLIENT_ID`, `WEB_CLIENT_ID`) and run `./build.sh`. The build adds the `oauth2` block to the Chrome manifest automatically.
3. While the app is in *Testing* status, only people you list as **Test users** (up to 100) can sign in, and they see an "unverified app" notice. To open it to anyone, submit the app for Google's OAuth verification. The calendar scope is "sensitive", so verification needs a privacy policy and a short demo video, but no paid security audit.

The manual client-ID setup stays available under *Manual setup* in Settings.

### If task sessions don't show up in Google Calendar
Click the status pill next to the date (or **Details** in the banner). The usual causes:
- **"Local only"**: Google isn't connected, so sessions only live in this browser. Connect it.
- **Permission needed**: the calendar checkbox was left unticked on Google's consent screen. Click **Reconnect** and tick it.
- **Sign-in expired**: some browsers block silent renewal after about an hour. Click **Reconnect**. With one-click Chrome sign-in this doesn't happen.
- **Wrong calendar hidden**: the panel shows which calendar sessions go to. In Google Calendar, make sure that calendar is ticked under *My calendars*, then refresh the page (Google Calendar doesn't live-update other tabs instantly).

Every sync also checks what is really on Google. Sessions that were deleted on the Google side are re-created, and duplicates are removed.

Task sessions are written with reminders, so they show on your phone and other people see you as busy. Edit tasks in Tempo: it overwrites changes made to those events in Google.
